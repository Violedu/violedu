# Market Scoring on the Four Criteria (Deep Analysis)

> Second step of the avatar-definition approach. Step 1 ("start from demand") is
> [demand-analysis.md](demand-analysis.md); this goes deep on Hormozi's four market
> criteria — **massive pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing** — and turns each
> into an evidence-backed score with a commit/kill decision. Feeds [customer-avatar.md](customer-avatar.md).

---

## How to read this (scoring rubric)

Hormozi's decision rule: rate each criterion **Great / Normal / Bad**.
- A **Bad** on any criterion stops the equation (don't enter) unless a **Great** elsewhere nullifies it.
- A **Normal** passes the buck to the next criterion.
- A **Great** on a higher-order criterion overpowers weaker ones.
- Order of importance: **market (these 4) > offer > persuasion.**

Each criterion below is broken into sub-dimensions, scored on evidence, with the gaps/risks that could move the score.

**Bottom line up front:** Pain **Great** · Purchasing power **Great** · Targetability **Great** · Growth **Good**. **Zero Bad ratings, three Greats → unambiguously commit-worthy.** The catch is not *whether* demand exists but its *narrowness* — which the scoring also quantifies.

---

## Criterion 1 — Massive Pain → **GREAT**

Hormozi: "They must not want, but desperately *need* it… the degree of pain is proportional to the price you can charge." Pain is assessed on four sub-dimensions:

| Sub-dimension | Assessment | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Intensity** | High | Music performance anxiety (MPA) affects **16.5–60% of professionals, 21–50% of students**, with one survey at **96% of undergrads** and **60–80% of professionals suffering *debilitating* forms**. The core fear your offer targets ("it falls apart on stage") is clinically widespread. |
| **Urgency** | High (audition segment) | A fixed audition/recital date converts chronic pain into an acute, deadline-bound need — the strongest buying state. |
| **Frequency** | High | Pain recurs every practice session (plateau, "am I improving?") and every performance — not a one-time itch. |
| **Painkiller vs. vitamin** | **Painkiller** | For the audition segment this is career-/place-defining, not a nice-to-have. Painkillers command premium pricing; vitamins don't. |

**The specific pains this market holds** (each maps to an offer element):
- *Achievement pain* — can't make the piece "feel ready"; the cadenza is a gamble. → roadmap, diagnosis.
- *Performance anxiety* — the MPA data above. → Performance Day Mental Blueprint bonus.
- *Feedback void* — "I need someone to actually watch me." → 48h video feedback, the core differentiator.
- *Plateau / isolation* — outgrew or lost a teacher. → 1-on-1 expert access.

**Risks to the score:** pain is intense but **narrow** — it only bites players already advanced enough to attempt the piece. And MPA prevalence figures vary wildly by methodology (self-reported, inconsistent definitions), so treat them as directional. Neither risk lowers the rating; both reinforce "small but desperate."

**Score: GREAT.** This is the strongest of the four criteria — a painkiller for a deadline-driven, status-laden fear.

## Criterion 2 — Purchasing Power → **GREAT**

Hormozi: "Your audience needs to be able to afford the service" (the unemployed-resume-service trap).

| Sub-dimension | Assessment | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Who pays** | Strong | Adults 20+ are **26.6% of music-education revenue and the fastest-growing age group (8.9% CAGR)**; audition-segment lessons are frequently **parent-funded**, where career stakes raise willingness to pay. |
| **Willingness to pay for 1-on-1** | Strong | One-to-one teaching still captures **~48% of all music-education spend** — people actively pay a premium for personalization over cheap/free content. |
| **Price benchmark** | Supportive | Advanced online lessons run **$60–130/hr**; your €80/session sits mid-market with headroom. |
| **Geographic tailwind** | Supportive | Rising middle-class incomes in Singapore, China, India, Brazil, Vietnam, with music in the **top-3 preferred** extracurricular spend — and online delivery makes that demand globally reachable. |

**Price elasticity read:** the audition segment is the *least* price-sensitive (the outcome is a seat/career), the recital/"Everest" segment is moderately sensitive (disposable-income hobby), and aspirational browsers are highly sensitive (they buy free content). Your screening should bias toward the first two.

**Risks to the score:** the *truly* qualified pool (advanced + can pay + has a deadline) is small; and adult amateurs treat this as discretionary spend that can be cut. Mitigated by global reach and by leading with the deadline-driven (inelastic) segment.

**Score: GREAT.** Money is present *and* demonstrably willing to flow to 1-on-1 specifically.

## Criterion 3 — Easy to Target → **GREAT**

Hormozi: look for avatars who "have associations, mailing lists, social-media groups, channels they all watch."

| Channel | Strength | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Aggregated platforms** | Very strong | **tonebase reports ~25k active / 100k+ total members** — proof this exact audience is already gathered in one place (and that someone monetizes them). |
| **Forums/communities** | Strong | r/violinist, violinist.com, Maestronet — high-intent, self-identifying. |
| **YouTube** | Strong | Large audiences on Tchaikovsky tutorials, cadenza lessons, practice vlogs, run-through reactions. |
| **Short-form** | Growing | TikTok **#classictok** and violin edits put the instrument in front of younger players daily. |
| **Search intent** | Present | "how to play the Tchaikovsky cadenza," "Tchaikovsky audition tips," "can I tackle the Tchaikovsky." |
| **Referral networks** | Strong | Teachers below this level with advancing students; conservatoire prep circles. |

**CAC intuition:** because the avatar self-identifies (they search and name the exact piece), intent-based targeting (search, YouTube, communities) should yield efficient, low-waste acquisition — far better than interrupting a broad audience. The existence of a profitable aggregator (tonebase) is the clearest signal that targeting works economically.

**Risks to the score:** the very narrowness that concentrates them also caps reach — you can find them easily, but there aren't millions. That's a volume ceiling, not a targeting failure.

**Score: GREAT.** Tight, self-identifying, already-aggregated audience with clear search intent.

---

## Criterion 4 — Growing → **GOOD** (Normal→Great)

Hormozi: "Growing markets are a tailwind; declining markets are a headwind" (the Lloyd/newspapers lesson).

| Layer | Direction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Online music education (category)** | Growing | ~**13–17%+ CAGR** across sources; one report projects the broad market **$20B → $101B (2025–2035) at 17.6% CAGR** (scope differs from the $4.27B figure in the demand doc — directional, not precise). |
| **Classical revival (top of funnel)** | Growing | Gen Z classical streaming **+50% since 2020**; **~65% of under-35s** listen to orchestral music regularly; Apple Music Classical did **$110M standalone in 2025**; 60% of Gen Z/Millennials say classical aids wellbeing. |
| **Adult / returning learners (your segment)** | Growing | Adults 20+ the **fastest-growing** education segment (8.9% CAGR); 1-on-1 still ~48% of spend. |

**The honest caveat that keeps this "Good," not "Great":** rising *listening* and *streaming* is not the same as rising demand to *learn an advanced concerto 1-on-1*. The revival widens the top of the funnel and the cultural tailwind, but the conversion from "listens to #classictok" → "pays €1,200 to master the Tchaikovsky" is indirect and unproven. The **adult-returner growth (8.9% CAGR) and the 1-on-1 spend share are the directly relevant signals**, and those are solidly positive.

**Score: GOOD.** A real tailwind at every layer; downgraded from Great only because the strongest growth numbers measure listening, not advanced learning.

## Cross-cut: which sub-segment scores highest

The four criteria don't score equally across the demand segments. This is what tells you *who to lead with*:

| Segment | Pain | Purchasing power | Targetable | Growing | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Audition / exam candidate** | Great (deadline + stakes) | Great (career stakes, parent-funded) | Great | Good | **Primary — highest urgency & willingness to pay** |
| **Recital / "Everest" adult** | Good (emotional, less urgent) | Great (discretionary, able) | Great | Good (8.9% CAGR) | **Secondary — year-round base** |
| **Aspirational browser** | Low (curiosity) | Bad (buys free content) | Great | Good | **Screen out — protects timeline & guarantee** |

**Read:** the market's four-criteria strength is concentrated in the **audition candidate**, with the **recital adult** as the steady base. The browser drags purchasing power to Bad — which is exactly why the free-call gate exists.

## Overall verdict & decision

| Criterion | Score | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| Massive pain | **Great** | Painkiller: deadline auditions + clinically common performance anxiety |
| Purchasing power | **Great** | Adults fastest-growing & able; ~48% of spend goes to 1-on-1 |
| Easy to target | **Great** | Self-identifying, already aggregated (tonebase 100k+), clear search intent |
| Growing | **Good** | Online-ed + classical revival + adult-returner tailwinds; listening ≠ learning caveat |

**Decision: COMMIT.** Three Greats and a Good, zero Bads — this clears Hormozi's bar comfortably. The market's only weakness is **narrowness, not quality**: small qualified pool, capped volume, discretionary for the recital segment. The strategic response is already identified — global reach to aggregate a thin niche, premium pricing on the inelastic audition segment, tight screening, and a repeat-revenue path to offset low single-piece LTV.

**What to watch (could change a score):**
- If you can't economically *reach* enough qualified buyers at scale, Targetability's "volume ceiling" risk becomes the binding constraint — test CAC early.
- If the offer drifts toward beginners to chase volume, Pain drops from painkiller to vitamin and pricing power collapses. Hold the entry bar.
