## How to use this doc

This is the single lens for every decision about copy, screening, and the offer. When you
write a headline, answer an objection, or decide whether to accept someone off the free
call — check it against this person.

**The load-bearing section is §3 (Entry Level).** The 12-week full-concerto promise is
only honest for a player who *already has the notes in all three movements and is
polishing*. Research is blunt here: learning movements II & III from scratch to
performance standard takes far longer than a quarter (a year+ for two movements from zero;
roughly a semester for movement I alone). If you let in someone below the entry bar, you
break the timeline and invite refunds against the 30-day guarantee. The avatar and the
screening exist to protect that promise.

## 3. Entry level — the load-bearing spec

The avatar must clear this bar for the 12-week full-concerto promise to be real. This is
exactly what the free call screens for.

**Must already be true:**
- Can play *through* all three movements — notes learned, even if rough, insecure, or
  under tempo. **They are polishing, not learning.**
- ~late Grade 8 / early performance-diploma level (ABRSM ARSM/LRSM, or equivalent).
- Secure shifting through the positions; reliable intonation framework; a working vibrato.
- Has performed at least one Romantic concerto movement publicly or in exam.
- Can commit 1.5–3 hrs of daily, structured practice for 12 weeks.

**If these aren't true,** the right move is to *decline or redirect* (see §10) — not to
sell a timeline the practice can't keep.

## 4. Dream outcome (↑ — increase, and frame via status)

| Layer | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Surface goal** | Perform the full Tchaikovsky concerto to performance standard. |
| **Real goal** | Win the recital / pass the diploma / place in the competition / clear the conservatoire audition. The piece is a personal Everest — proof to *themselves* they've arrived. |
| **Status goal (the real fuel)** | Have the panel stop taking notes. Have a teacher ask "what changed?" Have peers who used to outplay them go quiet. Be *the person who can play the Tchaikovsky.* |

**Copy rule (Hormozi's pro tip):** frame the payoff from *other people's* viewpoint.
Don't sell "the piece will feel ready" — sell the moment in front of the people whose
opinion she's chasing. This is currently the offer's weakest driver; this avatar tells you
exactly which status moment to write toward.

- Plateau anxiety: working hard and not visibly improving.
- Performance nerves that erase weeks of practice in four minutes.

## 7. Desires & buying triggers (why she buys *now*)

She doesn't buy from interest — she buys from a **deadline + a wall**. Watch for:

- A date on the calendar: recital, diploma exam, competition, conservatoire audition.
- A recent disappointing performance ("it fell apart and I don't know why").
- Her teacher is unavailable, retiring, or out of their depth on this piece.
- Plateau frustration boiling over — months of work, no breakthrough.
- Comparison sting — a peer just performed it well.

**Implication:** lead generation and copy should hook the *deadline* and the *recent
failure*. No deadline → weak urgency → likely not your buyer right now.

## 10. Offer-fit via the value equation

`Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)`

- **Dream outcome (↑):** frame toward the status moment in §4 — the panel, the teacher,
  the peers. This is the highest-ROI copy change. The avatar tells you *which* moment.
- **Perceived likelihood (↑):** this avatar makes proof concrete — collect named results
  ("X passed their LRSM," "Y placed at [competition]"), before/after clips. The screening
  call itself raises perceived likelihood ("I only take you if I'm confident it works").
  Keep the Week-1 diagnosis, Week-11 mock, and guarantee front and centre.
- **Time delay (↓):** 12 weeks is honest **only at the §3 entry level** — defend that bar.
  Keep front-loading fast wins (a fix on the free call, the day-7 written diagnosis).
- **Effort & sacrifice (↓):** lean on the Roadmap and done-with-you positioning —
  "you never wonder what to practice; zero wasted hours."

## 11. Free-call screening checklist (the gate)

The free 30-min call is not just a sales call — it's the gate that protects the 12-week
promise and the guarantee.

**Accept when ALL are true:**
- ☐ Plays through all three movements (rough is fine — notes are *there*).
- ☐ ~late Grade 8 / diploma level; has performed a Romantic concerto.
- ☐ Can commit 1.5–3 hrs/day for 12 weeks.
- ☐ Has a real reason/deadline (recital, exam, competition, audition).

**Decline or redirect when:**
- ☐ Can't yet play through the movements → this is *learning*, not polishing; 12 weeks is
  a fantasy. Redirect to a longer/foundational path or a different repertoire goal.
- ☐ Under ~1 hr/day practice → can't consolidate the material; outcome will miss.
- ☐ No deadline and no clear goal → low urgency, low completion odds; revisit later.
- ☐ Below the technical bar → set expectations honestly; offer the right next step instead.

Saying "not yet" to the wrong buyer is what lets you keep saying "12 weeks" to the right
one.
